W7｜Pre-training: Scaling Laws and Data Curation

Part II — Training · Pre-training：scaling laws 與資料策展

Where This Fits

Part II opens here. Six weeks have taken a Transformer apart until every layer has a cost: W1’s ledger wrote C_{\text{train}} \approx 6ND and had you compute what a 7B model costs at D \approx 20N; W4 counted N matrix by matrix; W6 split N into N_{\text{total}} and N_{\text{active}}. This week asks what that formula does not answer: given a compute budget C, how should N and D be split? — and who said 20, on what grounds. The first half is one of the course’s three mathematical peaks: put Chinchilla’s fitted form \mathcal{L}(N, D) = E + AN^{-\alpha} + BD^{-\beta} under the constraint 6ND = C, derive the first-order condition on the board, and see that the optimum is where the two marginal returns are equal, that the exponent \beta/(\alpha+\beta) is the structure and 20 is only a fitted constant; then read two 2024 papers that reconcile Kaplan’s N^* \propto C^{0.73} with Chinchilla’s C^{0.50} — the gap comes from how N is counted (W4’s “which N”), from warmup, and from re-tuning the hyperparameters at every size — the first time “a scaling law is a fit, not a measurement” is taught as methodology. The 2026 message is unchanged: Chinchilla has not been overturned, but its question has been rewritten — from “optimal under one training-compute budget” to “optimal under several cost axes”: inference (Sardana 2024 → Roberts 2026, which puts the number of test-time samples into the same budget), precision (Kumar), the data ceiling (Muennighoff), plus W6’s “which N does a scaling law mean for an MoE” (Clark, Abnar) and W2’s V (Tao). Every added axis moves the optimum, and the models shipped in 2026 are almost all overtrained — not against Chinchilla, but optimising a different objective. The second half is data: compute decides how many tokens to buy; who decides which tokens, and how? One public, reproducible pipeline (Common Crawl → extraction → language identification → quality filtering → deduplication → decontamination → mixing and ordering), each stage with a failure mode; the verbatim copy in W1’s n = 8 generation gets its answer here (deduplication); MMLU appears for the first time here, and so, in the same breath, do contamination and the error rate of its questions (W14 develops both); W2’s Traditional Chinese data gap looks for its place among FineWeb2’s thousand-plus languages — and finds that it has no subset of its own: GlotLID sorts Han characters into one script (cmn_Hani, no Traditional/Simplified split), Hakka hak_Hani has 74 documents and Taiwanese nan_Latn 2,891 (dataset card, read 2026-10-26). It is also a week of training in reading evidence: synthetic data is taught as a pair — Rephrasing the Web (2024, optimistic) next to BeyondWeb (2025, the same first author’s negative result); model collapse assumes that new data replace old data while practice accumulates them, and Gerstgrasser et al. prove that accumulation does not collapse, so it is taught as “a theoretical claim stronger than its evidence”, not as consensus. The compute-optimal arithmetic is run live by the instructor, the small-scale fit and its extrapolation are rehearsed in advance and replayed, and MinHash is written live; you do not need to run anything.

Learning Objectives

  1. On the board, derive the first-order condition from \mathcal{L}(N, D) = E + AN^{-\alpha} + BD^{-\beta} and 6ND = C; state what the optimum means (the marginal returns of N and D are equal), N^{*} \propto C^{\beta/(\alpha+\beta)} and D^{*} \propto C^{\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)}; explain why D/N \approx 20 is a fitted constant while the exponent is the structure; and say what each of Chinchilla’s three estimation methods holds fixed.
  2. Explain where the gap between Kaplan (N^{*} \propto C^{0.73}) and Chinchilla (C^{0.50}) comes from — whether N counts the embeddings and the last layer’s compute (W4’s “which N”), warmup length, and whether the optimizer is re-tuned at every scale — and state Besiroglu et al.’s three charges against the original Chinchilla fit (inconsistent with the first two methods, fails to fit the extracted data, confidence intervals implausibly narrow); say what “Chinchilla was not overturned but rewritten” means.
  3. Write each 2024–2026 correction as “one term added to the cost” and re-derive the first-order condition: inference-aware (6ND + 2N D_{\text{inf}}; the inference term taxes N only → a smaller N and a larger D; Roberts’s T^2 puts the number of samples k into the same budget), precision-aware (N_{\text{eff}} = N \cdot g(P); post-training-quantization degradation grows with training data), data-constrained (D_{\text{eff}} saturates with epochs; up to about 4 epochs are nearly free); for an MoE, say which N a scaling law means (6N_{\text{active}}D for compute, N_{\text{total}} for capacity; Clark’s effective parameter count, Abnar’s optimal sparsity), and for V, recall W2’s derivation.
  4. List five methodological reasons a scaling-law fit goes wrong, with one paper each: too few points to extrapolate from (the (Mis)Fitting checklist); the collinear design D = kN that makes the coefficients of N and D unidentifiable (Kricheli); hyperparameters not re-tuned at each size (Porian; muP is one answer); a learning-rate schedule tied to the training length (Hägele’s and MiniCPM’s constant + cooldown); loss and downstream ability not lying on one curve (Gadre’s power law against Schaeffer’s metric argument) — and point to which one is happening in demo 2’s fit.
  5. Describe each stage of a public, reproducible pre-training data pipeline (a Common Crawl snapshot → extraction → language identification → quality filtering, rules against a classifier → deduplication, MinHash/LSH → decontamination, n-gram overlap → PII), give one failure mode and one public reference per stage (FineWeb/FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, Dolma, Nemotron-CC, FineWeb2), and write the one line by which MinHash estimates Jaccard and what the LSH band parameters tune.
  6. Explain the state of data mixing and ordering: small-scale proxies that predict the mixture (DoReMi, RegMix, DataDecide, Shukor) and their still-open transferability; mid-training/annealing that puts high-quality and domain data into the cooldown (OLMo 2’s Dolmino; Blakeney’s domain upsampling); and the other half of the recipe — stability (OLMo 2 §3’s reordered norm, QK-norm and z-loss; Kimi K2’s MuonClip with zero spikes) — as the precondition for a small-scale fit to extrapolate at all.
  7. State the known limits of synthetic data in pre-training (Rephrasing the Web → BeyondWeb’s “no silver bullet” → Niklaus’s 2026 systematic study), separate the theoretical assumption of model collapse (replacement) from practice (accumulation; Gerstgrasser et al. prove a finite bound under accumulation), and, the first time MMLU is mentioned, say contamination and the 6.49% error rate of its questions (Gema et al.) in the same breath.
