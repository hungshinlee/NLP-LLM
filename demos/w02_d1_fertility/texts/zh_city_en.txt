At half past six in the morning, the city has not fully woken up. The breakfast shop at the mouth of the lane has already turned its lights on; the owner fries egg pancakes while chatting with regulars about last night's typhoon news. In the MRT station, commuters look down at their phones: some are reading the news, some are replying to messages, and some just close their eyes, hoping to steal a few more minutes of sleep. Beside the bus stop, an old man slowly folds his umbrella and looks up at the sky: the clouds are thick, but the wind has died down. For many people this is just another ordinary Wednesday; but for a student who has just moved to this city, every corner looks like an unfinished sentence, waiting for them to fill in the rest.
